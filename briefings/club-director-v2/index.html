01 / Final product · Core scope

A sandbox for football decisions

Passing is the first application. The intended scope extends to carries and set pieces.

Open play

Explore alternative passes and carries from the same match situation.

Set pieces

Test choices in free kicks, corners and penalties.

Player responses

Compare plausible movements and continuations after the changed action.

Decision trade-offs

Assess what an option could create, its execution risk and the cost of losing possession.

Final-product vision. Each action type needs its own data, development and validation.

02 / Proposed use

How a club could use it

  1. Select a moment

    Find a situation from a match, team or player.

  2. Replay the decision

    Review the footage and player positions.

  3. Change and play

    Edit an action and play through possible continuations.

  4. Review the choices

    Discuss the trade-offs with the coach or player.

Analysts prepare scenarios; coaches use them in reviews. A club-controlled player portal could support individual reflection.

Proposed workflow. The full simulation and player portal remain to be developed.

Appendix B / Proposed model

Separate success, value and selection

Pass success

Estimate P(completion | pre-action state, intended target, pass type). Extend the existing logistic and boosted-tree research; calibrate on held-out matches.

Pass value

Estimate expected change in team possession value, including unsuccessful branches and opponent threat. Use a fixed horizon and common units for every option.

Pass selection

Estimate which feasible action a player tends to choose. Use it to assess support and context; a popular action is not automatically the best action.

Score candidate actions from the same information available before the pass. Realised end points and later events cannot become input features.

Appendix C / Plausibility

A pass estimate is only the first stage

Feasible action set

Check player reach, ball speed, pass type, available time, offside and interception geometry. Allow uncertainty in body orientation and intended recipient.

Short response model

With continuous tracking, learn joint player and ball responses conditioned on the edited action. Generate several possible continuations over a short horizon.

Abstention

Reject unsupported edits and flag missing coverage. Do not freeze defenders while moving the ball, or show one continuation as what would have happened.

Unplayed alternatives have no observed ground truth. Observational fit alone cannot establish their causal outcomes.

Appendix F / Data rights

Access and training rights must be explicit

Scope of use

Confirm research, commercial training, fine-tuning, evaluation, inference and use of derived models. Club subscriptions may restrict each differently.

Sharing and retention

Agree whether clips, features, weights and outputs can be shared; specify hosting, subcontractors, security, retention and deletion.

Ownership and people

Identify the rights holder for events, tracking and footage. Review player data, academy data, privacy obligations and any restrictions on identification.

Written record

Maintain a source register with contract version, allowed uses, expiry and attribution. Obtain a qualified contract review before committing to a commercial dataset.

Model code, weights, training data and match footage can carry different terms. An open download is not a complete permission record.

Appendix H / Validation

What we need to prove

Prediction

Split by match and time; retain an external team or competition test. Measure Brier score, log loss, calibration and uncertainty, with simple baselines.

Value and support

Use a fixed horizon; include opponent risk. Audit action coverage and hidden confounding. Selection adjustment cannot create evidence for unseen actions.

Simulation

Check held-out trajectories, physical violations, distribution coverage and error by horizon. Coaches assess football plausibility separately.

Usefulness

Compare review time and coaching usefulness on matched tasks. Record rejected suggestions and repeat use; agree targets before examining results.

A pass model’s accuracy or AUC cannot prove that the best alternative has been identified.
