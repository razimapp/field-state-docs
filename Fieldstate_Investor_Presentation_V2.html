APPENDIX · THE MATH

How the model turns inputs into a probability.

STEP 1
Standardise
x'ᵢ = (xᵢ − μᵢ) / sᵢ
Each input is rescaled using the mean and spread measured across the training data, so no single feature dominates just because of its units.
STEP 2
Combine
z = b + Σ wᵢ·x'ᵢ
Every one of the 47 standardised inputs is multiplied by its own learned weight, then added together with a starting baseline, b.
STEP 3
Convert
P = 1 / (1 + e⁻ᶻ)
The combined score z is squeezed into a number between 0 and 1 — the completion probability shown on screen.

z is a raw score with no fixed range. The sigmoid in step 3 is what turns it into a genuine probability.

APPENDIX · TRAINING METHOD

The weights are learned, not hand-picked.

METHOD
Regularised logistic regression, fit once on the full corpus. Chosen over a black-box model because it gives a direct, auditable probability — every one of the 47 weights can be inspected individually.
VALIDATION
Tested with match-grouped cross-validation — a whole match stays on one side of the split, never both — plus leave-one-competition-out testing: train on four competitions, score the fifth, unseen one.
DEPLOYMENT
Coefficients are frozen after training into a versioned artifact. The model does not retrain when someone opens a scenario — every user sees the same, tested weights.

No balanced class weighting is used — the shown probability reflects the true completion rate, not an artificially rebalanced one.
