Why passes fail

20,551 failed passes, 18.09% of everything tested, broken down by recorded reason.

Failed passes are not all the same football problem: some are cut out, some are misplaced, some run out of play, and some fail because the receiving run or timing breaks down. The current event data records the outcome label, not every tactical cause.

“Incomplete” is a broad event-data label. It may cover things like a misplaced ball, an interception, or a miscontrol. The data doesn’t yet separate which.

What this evidence supports, and what it doesn’t

Safe to say
  • ✓A real, measurable pass-completion signal read from event data, not noise
  • ✓Materially sharper than guessing, and sharper than judging passes on length alone
  • ✓Calibrated enough to show a bounded pass-completion estimate for a specified pass
  • ✓Enough evidence to fund the next stage: richer data, more features, what-if possession modelling
Not yet safe to say
  • ✕Which pass was the “best” one
  • ✕What a player intended
  • ✕What definitely happens next in the match
  • ✕The exact reason a pass labelled “Incomplete” broke down

Why this matters: Fieldstate has ranking power. It usually scores passes that reach a teammate above passes that break down. That is enough to start comparing recorded vs. edited pass choices with evidence, not guesswork.
